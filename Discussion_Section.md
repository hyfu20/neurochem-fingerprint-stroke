# Discussion

In this nationwide multicenter study of 3,582 patients with acute ischemic stroke, we assembled a system-level neurochemical–inflammatory prognostic atlas that systematically charts where, and under which inflammatory conditions, neurochemical disconnection acquires prognostic value beyond conventional structural metrics. Three principal findings emerged. First, neurochemical disruption at discharge was robustly associated with functional disability independently of lesion volume and corticospinal tract disconnection (12 of 17 systems retained significance after global FDR correction in Model C, and the same 12 also remained significant after local BH-FDR correction within the 17-NT family in CST-adjusted Model D, qₙₜ < 0.05, Fig. 2); after orthogonalization for lesion volume and adjustment for stroke severity, however, no individual neurochemical system independently predicted 12-month outcome (Supplementary Fig. S2). Second, neurochemical disruption acquired prognostic significance primarily when it co-occurred with acute systemic inflammation, defining a joint dual-burden phenotype (combined high neurochemical damage plus high inflammation) that significantly improved risk reclassification (continuous NRI = +0.35 [+0.08, +0.43], P = 0.002; IDI = +0.009 [+0.003, +0.017], P < 0.001; Fig. 3); the absolute-risk pattern was approximately additive rather than super-additive on the additive-OR scale (Fig. 3B; RERI confidence interval crossing zero across all seven globally-FDR-significant pairs). Third, the small-lesion, severe-outcome phenotype was characterized by disproportionate cholinergic tract damage coupled with elevated IL-6 (Fig. 6), spatially anchoring the dual-burden phenotype to a specific neuroanatomical substrate. Together, these results position the present work as a hypothesis-generating prognostic atlas rather than a mechanistic dissection of any single causal pathway.

A central finding of this study is that neurochemical disruption, on its own, does not determine long-term outcome. At first glance this appears inconsistent with our own acute-phase associations and with those reported by Koch et al.^5 We interpret the result, however, as evidence of a conditional mechanism: certain transmitter hubs, particularly within the noradrenergic and cholinergic systems, function as immunoregulatory switches that hold peripheral inflammation under tonic restraint.^60–62 When a stroke lesion spares these systems, anti-inflammatory capacity is preserved, peripheral inflammation is buffered, and lesion size carries most of the prognostic weight. When the infarct disrupts these switches, however, this restraint is lost. The resulting unopposed inflammation, rather than constituting a separate second hit, may represent a downstream consequence of disabling the brain's own anti-inflammatory circuitry,^8,9 contributing to the transition from an acute neurological deficit to chronic functional disability. Consistent with this conditional-mechanism reading rather than with a direct causal cascade, two complementary sensitivity analyses converged on the same conclusion: bootstrap mediation analysis (Supplementary Fig. S5) found no consistent indirect effect through baseline IL-6 across the 17 neurotransmitter systems, and an *a priori* longitudinal test in the hsCRP three-time-point sub-cohort (Supplementary Fig. S7; N = 1,334) found no Resid_CHA × hsCRP-resolution-slope interaction (LR χ²₁ = 0.001, P = 0.98) and a sub-additive RERI of −0.63 (95% CI −1.94, +0.32) for the dichotomous Resid_CHA × sustained-hsCRP phenotype. The cross-sectional mediation null and the longitudinal interaction null are mechanistically distinct (mediation tests a causal pathway in cross-section; the trajectory analysis tests whether cholinergic disconnection slows the resolution of post-stroke inflammation) but they converge on the same conclusion: the joint phenotype operates predominantly through effect modification of two independent prognostic axes rather than through a uniform causal-mediation pathway or a coupled "release-the-brake → sustained inflammation" cascade.

This conditional-mechanism reading is biologically plausible in light of the canonical cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, in which tonic vagal outflow restrains peripheral cytokine production through a multi-synaptic circuit involving the splenic nerve and α7 nicotinic receptors on splenic macrophages (Tracey 2002; Rosas-Ballina 2008; Hoover 2017). Strategic disconnection of cholinergic and noradrenergic tracts, even within small lesions, may release this immune brake and render patients disproportionately sensitive to inflammatory insults that would otherwise be effectively buffered (Endres 2022; Bharani 2017; Song 2019). Within our atlas framework, this provides a parsimonious biological rationale for why the dual-burden phenotype is concentrated on cholinergic and noradrenergic systems; we do not, however, claim to have dissected the underlying causal chain in the present observational design, and the mediation analysis (Supplementary Fig. S5) does not support a uniform NT → IL-6 → outcome cascade as the dominant mechanism.

Critically, the lack of super-additive synergy on the absolute-risk scale (RERI confidence intervals crossing zero across all seven globally-FDR-significant pairs; Supplementary Table S4) indicates that the joint phenotype delineates the highest-risk subgroup through the co-occurrence of two prognostic axes whose combined absolute risk does not exceed the sum of their individual contributions (de Mutsert 2009; Knol 2012). This pattern is consistent with effect modification on the multiplicative scale, whereby neurochemical disruption amplifies the relative but not the absolute excess risk attributable to inflammation (VanderWeele 2009; VanderWeele 2019), and does not require invoking a separate discrete synergistic step. We acknowledge, however, that the bootstrap-based RERI confidence intervals may have been underpowered to detect modest super-additive interaction, and this null result on the additive scale should be confirmed in larger independent cohorts. Accordingly, the dual-burden label introduced here should be read as a descriptive shorthand for the co-occurrence of two prognostic risk axes rather than as a claim of emergent biological synergy.

Our cross-validation analysis (10-fold stratified CV across all 17 NT systems; max per-NT ΔAUC = +0.0015 [α4β2], all NT ΔAUC < +0.002 in held-out data; Fig. 4B) and decision curve analysis (Fig. 4A) transparently demonstrate that the dual-burden phenotype does not meaningfully improve individual-level prognostication beyond the established clinical gold standard of NIHSS + lesion volume + age + sex. This is consistent with the broader stroke-biomarker literature, in which few neuroimaging-derived markers have incrementally improved upon NIHSS for long-term stroke outcome prediction (Coutureau 2021; Sperber 2023).

We emphasize that the primary contribution of this work is atlas-based mechanism mapping rather than incremental prediction, a positioning explicitly shared by Koch et al.^5, who likewise framed neurotransmitter-informed connectivity mapping as a tool for understanding stroke heterogeneity rather than as an incremental gain over established clinical scales. NIHSS indicates that a patient is likely to do poorly; the atlas indicates which molecular systems are compromised and under which inflammatory conditions this compromise becomes clinically consequential.

This distinction has direct implications for therapeutic target identification. While NIHSS by itself cannot guide molecular intervention, mapping a specific neuro-immune vulnerability (e.g., cholinergic tract × hsCRP, DAT × IL-6) provides a starting point for biomarker-stratified trials of anti-inflammatory or neuromodulatory therapy in stroke recovery. The ESTREL trial (N = 610), the largest randomised study of dopaminergic augmentation in stroke rehabilitation, found no benefit of levodopa in unselected patients and explicitly recommended that dopaminergic augmentation should not be used routinely outside of mechanistic or biomarker-stratified trials (Engelter 2025), a recommendation that aligns directly with the framework proposed here. Conversely, vagus nerve stimulation paired with rehabilitation has shown efficacy for upper-limb motor recovery in the VNS-REHAB trial (Dawson 2021), but was deployed without stratification by cholinergic tract integrity, a candidate selection criterion that neurochemical fingerprinting could help operationalise in future trials.

This study has several strengths. The cohort (N = 3,582) is substantially larger than prior neurochemical disconnectome studies in stroke (e.g., N = 126 in Koch et al.^5), providing the statistical power required to detect moderate effects under stringent multiple-comparison control (global FDR); this is a relevant consideration given that voxel-based lesion-deficit mappings have been shown to be unstable when N < 100 (Lorca-Puls 2018). The graded covariate-adjustment scheme (Models A–D) addresses confounding in a structured, stepwise manner; importantly, the survival of neurochemical effects after additional control for weighted corticospinal-tract (CST) lesion load, a validated imaging biomarker of motor outcome (Feng 2015; Stinear 2017), argues against a residual motor-proxy artefact. The structural-orthogonalization step is effective in resolving extreme TLV–load collinearity (VIF > 700 → ~1). The inclusion of inflammatory biomarkers, whose independent prognostic value in this same CNSR-III cohort has been separately validated (Li 2022; Jia 2024), permits hypothesis-driven mechanistic exploration rather than purely associative analysis. Finally, the explicit reporting of null results (Supplementary Fig. S2 for independent 12-month effects; Supplementary Fig. S5 for IL-6 mediation) and predictive limitations (Fig. 4: CV and DCA) reinforces rather than weakens the atlas framing of this study: the absence of independent 12-month effects combined with significant interaction effects supports a conditional rather than a direct mechanism, consistent with a systematic survey of prognostic effect modification rather than a single-pathway causal dissection.

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, the normative neurotransmitter atlas maps represent group-mean receptor densities from healthy individuals and therefore cannot capture lesion-induced or recovery-related receptor remodelling, a phenomenon directly demonstrated by post-stroke PET studies in which 5-HT2 receptor density was reduced by 30–49% in non-lesioned cortex, with the magnitude depending on the subcortical extension of the lesion (Véra 1996). Nevertheless, the atlas-based strategy has been independently validated in multiple stroke cohorts^5,7 (Alves 2025) and remains the only feasible approach at the present scale in the absence of individual PET imaging. Second, the clinical 2D thick-slice T1 acquisitions precluded nonlinear registration; despite cost-function masking, some residual spatial inaccuracy cannot be excluded, although a recent head-to-head comparison of registration strategies on similar clinical FLAIR data found that nonlinear registration does not consistently outperform affine approaches and can itself introduce image distortion (Jühling 2024). Third, the interaction analyses, although FDR-corrected, are exploratory; the cross-sectional design does not permit definitive causal ordering between neurochemical damage and baseline inflammation. Fourth, CNSR-III enrolled exclusively Chinese patients, whose stroke aetiology distribution differs from Western populations, with a higher proportion of large-artery atherosclerosis and intracranial atherosclerotic disease and a lower cardioembolic fraction (Tsai 2013; Wu 2019), and external validation in ethnically diverse cohorts will therefore be needed before extrapolating these findings. Fifth, the inflammatory biomarker panel is not symmetric across time: IL-6 was protocol-defined as a baseline-only measurement, whereas hsCRP was repeated at 3 and 12 months (Supplementary Fig. S7). For IL-6 we therefore could not test whether cholinergic disconnection slows the cytokine resolution phase that experimental and modelling evidence places between 6 h and 24 h post-stroke (Arishi 2026), and a single early time-point may misclassify patients whose IL-6 trajectory diverges between admission and the subacute window. For hsCRP, by contrast, the three-time-point analysis revealed no Resid_CHA × resolution-slope interaction (Supplementary Fig. S7) and so corroborates the effect-modification reading on a longitudinal scale; we cannot rule out, however, that an interaction would emerge in the sub-acute window (24 h to 7 d) that lies between admission and the 3-month repeat, and prospective cohorts with denser early sampling will be required to settle this point. Finally, individual effect sizes, including the per-NT cross-validated gains (all ΔAUC < +0.002 in held-out data), are modest by the standard of single-biomarker discovery, consistent with a broader stroke-biomarker literature in which blood-based markers have not delivered consistent incremental prognostic value beyond NIHSS (Montellano 2021). The contribution of the present work lies in systematically charting which neurochemical systems acquire prognostic significance, and under which inflammatory conditions, rather than in delivering a single high-effect-size biomarker; the dual-burden phenotype identified within this atlas is therefore a hypothesis-generating starting point that awaits prospective validation before clinical implementation.

Three directions for follow-up work appear most informative. First, prospective validation of the dual-burden phenotype in an independent cohort with serial inflammatory biomarker measurements densely sampled across the sub-acute window — including early time-points (6–24 h) that capture the experimentally and computationally characterised post-stroke cytokine peak (Arishi 2026) and that lie between the admission and 3-month measurements available in CNSR-III — will be required to test whether cholinergic disconnection delays cytokine resolution in a phase not resolved by the present hsCRP trajectory analysis (Supplementary Fig. S7), and whether the converging mediation- and trajectory-null results reported here represent a genuine independence of the two prognostic axes or a sampling artefact of the available time-points. Second, individual-level PET–MRI studies in stroke patients are needed to benchmark the normative-atlas approach^7 against subject-specific receptor maps and to quantify the magnitude of lesion-induced receptor remodelling that group-mean atlases necessarily smooth over. Third, the specific NT × inflammation interactions identified here motivate biomarker-stratified clinical trials, for instance testing whether targeted anti-inflammatory therapy (e.g., IL-6 receptor blockade) or vagus nerve stimulation (Dawson 2021) preferentially benefits patients with high neurochemical vulnerability scores, an approach directly aligned with recent calls for biomarker-guided patient selection in stroke neurorehabilitation (Engelter 2025).
