# Figure Legends

## Figure 1

**Figure 1 | High-throughput neurochemical lesion mapping pipeline and case illustration.**

**(A)** Raw diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI, *b* = 1000) of a representative patient with a strategic small infarct (total lesion volume = 816 mm³). **(B)** Automated ischemic core segmentation via the DeepISLES ensemble model (red contour) in native space. **(C)** Lesion mask registered to MNI152 standard space using cost-function masking (FLIRT). **(D–D″)** Individual neurochemical fingerprinting: the standardized lesion mask (cyan contour) is spatially intersected with normative PET-derived neurotransmitter density maps (inferno colormap) for the noradrenergic (D, NAT), cholinergic (D′, α4β2), and serotonergic (D″, 5-HT₁ₐ) systems. Despite the minimal lesion volume, the infarct strategically transects high-density hubs of the noradrenergic and cholinergic networks, while the serotonergic system is relatively spared. This disproportionate chemoarchitectural disruption provides a molecular-level explanation for the patient's unfavorable long-term outcome (3-month mRS = 3).

## Figure 2

**Figure 2 | Neurotransmitter effects survive corticospinal tract control.**

**(A)** Forest plot showing odds ratios (OR, 95% CI) for all 17 neurotransmitter systems predicting discharge mRS in the fully adjusted model (Model C: Resid_NT + TLV + NIHSS + Age + Sex). Systems reaching FDR-corrected significance (q < 0.05) are highlighted. **(B)** OR attenuation analysis after additional covariate control for corticospinal tract load (Model D). For each significant system, the percentage of effect retained after CST control is shown. Over 80% of the neurochemical prognostic effect was independent of motor tract disconnection, confirming that neurotransmitter mapping captures a prognostically distinct dimension of stroke injury. Error bars represent 95% confidence intervals from ordinal logistic regression. All tests are two-sided.

## Figure 3

**Figure 3 | Neuro-immune dual-burden interaction analysis.**

**(A)** Heatmap of FDR-corrected interaction P-values (−log₁₀ q) for all 17 NT × 2 inflammatory biomarker pairs (IL-6, hsCRP) predicting 12-month mRS. Significant interactions (q < 0.05) are outlined. **(B)** Dual-burden interaction analysis presented as two complementary sub-panels. **Left:** Four-cell additive interaction (Knol & VanderWeele 2012) for the most significant pair (medial cholinergic pathway × hsCRP, q_global = 0.011): patients are stratified by median splits of TLV-orthogonalized NT damage (Resid_NT) and the inflammatory marker into LL (reference, low NT × low marker), LH (low NT × high marker), HL (high NT × low marker) and HH (high NT × high marker). Bars show observed 12-month poor-outcome (mRS > 2) risk per cell with Wilson 95% CI; the dashed line marks the additive expectation under no interaction (P_LL + (P_LH − P_LL) + (P_HL − P_LL)). The HH cell carried the highest absolute risk (0.32 [95% CI 0.29–0.36]) but did not significantly exceed the additive expectation (0.31), indicating that the joint phenotype reflects co-occurrence of two independent risk axes rather than super-additive synergy. The covariate-adjusted Relative Excess Risk due to Interaction (RERI) with 95% CI (1,000-iteration bootstrap), Attributable Proportion (AP), Synergy Index (S) and the multiplicative-scale 1-df likelihood-ratio test are annotated in the sub-panel title (numerical values in Table_S3_AdditiveInteraction_summary.csv; full seven-pair table in Table_S4_AdditiveInteraction_AllSigPairs.csv). **Right:** Simple-slope visualization of the *a priori* cholinergic–IL-6 dual-burden model used in panels C–D: 12-month mRS regressed on z-scored IL-6 (adjusted for TLV, NIHSS, Age and Sex) separately within each tertile of TLV-orthogonalized CHA damage (Resid_human_CHA). The inflammation slope progressively attenuates as CHA damage increases (per-tertile slopes and P-values in Table_S5_SimpleSlope_CHA_IL6.csv), reproducing the sub-multiplicative pattern on the continuous inflammation scale and bridging to the prediction analyses in panels (C)–(D). **(C)** Incremental predictive performance: receiver operating characteristic curves comparing the clinical base model (TLV + NIHSS + Age + Sex; AUC = 0.718), the NT-only model (ΔAUC = +0.000, P = 0.62; NRI = +0.027, P = 0.39), and the *a priori* cholinergic–IL-6 dual-burden model (AUC = 0.728; ΔAUC = +0.010 [+0.004, +0.020], P < 0.001; continuous NRI = +0.351 [+0.082, +0.434], P = 0.002; IDI = +0.009 [+0.003, +0.017], P < 0.001). **(D)** Calibration plot for the dual-burden model showing agreement between predicted and observed probabilities. Shaded regions indicate 95% confidence intervals. All statistical tests are two-sided.

## Figure 5

**Figure 5 | Neuro-immune vulnerability atlas (NIVA) and validation.**

**(A)** Composite NIVA map projected onto MNI152 standard space. Fourteen PET-derived neurotransmitter density maps were weighted by −log₁₀(global FDR q) of their empirical NT × inflammation interaction (capped at 5); NT systems failing global FDR (q ≥ 0.05) received zero weight and were excluded from the composite (6 of 14 NT systems contributed). The weighted sum was thresholded at the 80th percentile. Hot colours indicate regions of maximal neuro-immune convergence, concentrated in basal ganglia, thalamus, brainstem, and medial prefrontal/insular cortex. **(B)** Split-half cross-validation. Patients were randomly partitioned into two halves; the covariate-adjusted OLS NT × inflammation interaction pipeline was independently refitted in each half and a half-specific NIVA was reconstructed from scratch, gated at raw P < 0.05 per half. The raw-P criterion matches the per-replicate threshold used for the bootstrap panel (Fig. 5C; Hansen et al., 2022): both familywise (Bonferroni) and BH-FDR corrections are statistically under-powered for an interaction-of-interaction term at the reduced n ≈ 1,800 of each half and routinely collapse to zero survivors in one half, whereas voxel-wise concordance between two halves independently meeting raw P < 0.05 constitutes a stringent test of spatial reproducibility in its own right. Left: voxel-wise concordance (hexbin density; Pearson r reported on the plot). Centre/right: axial top-20% slices (z = +5 mm) of the two half-NIVAs. **(C)** Bootstrap NT-weight stability. Patients were resampled with replacement 200 times; in each replicate the covariate-adjusted interaction pipeline was rerun and NT weights were obtained at raw P < 0.05 (re-running BH-FDR inside every bootstrap is known to introduce additional Monte-Carlo variability for borderline NTs; we therefore used raw P per replicate and report selection frequency as the stability metric, following Hansen et al., 2022). Bars show bootstrap mean −log₁₀(P) ± 95% CI per NT; the number to the right of each bar is the selection frequency. Crimson bars = robustly significant (selected in ≥80% of replicates); orange = intermittently significant; grey = never significant. Dashed line marks the per-replicate P = 0.05 threshold (−log₁₀ 0.05 ≈ 1.30). **(D)** Voxel-wise spatial correlation between the NIVA and each constituent Hansen PET atlas. Bars colour-coded by full-sample FDR contribution to the NIVA anchor (crimson: q < 0.05, contributed; light blue: q ≥ 0.05, excluded). Note that the FDR contribution shown here (full-sample, single-fit BH ranking) differs from the bootstrap stability shown in panel (C): a few NT systems with high bootstrap selection frequency (notably human_CHA) sit just past the full-sample FDR cut-off and therefore receive zero weight in the FDR-anchored NIVA, producing a low spatial correlation in this panel despite their bootstrap stability: the two panels are intentionally complementary stability metrics.

## Figure 6

**Figure 6 | Small-lesion severe-outcome phenotype: neurochemical mechanism.**

**(A)** Among patients with small lesions (TLV < Q1, N = 894), 17.7% had severe outcomes (mRS ≥ 3). The severe group showed significantly higher cholinergic tract damage (Medial_Path: Cohen's d = 0.268, P = 0.002; Lateral_Path: d = 0.220, P = 0.014) compared to the good-outcome group. **(B)** The severe group also had elevated baseline IL-6 (d ≈ 0.55, P < 0.001), consistent with loss of the cholinergic anti-inflammatory brake. **(C)** Proposed mechanistic chain: strategic cholinergic disconnection → loss of anti-inflammatory capacity → unopposed systemic inflammation → poor functional recovery. All P-values are from two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests with Bonferroni correction.

## Supplementary Figure 1

**Supplementary Figure 1 | Patient screening and enrollment flowchart.**

Flow diagram detailing the sequential selection of patients from the Third China National Stroke Registry (CNSR-III; N = 15,166). Clinical exclusions included transient ischemic attack (n = 1,020), hemorrhagic transformation (n = 193), and stroke recurrence within 3 months (n = 944). Patients with moderate stroke severity (admission NIHSS 5–15) and available DWI data (N = 4,050) underwent automated lesion segmentation using the DeepISLES deep learning model (segmentation failure, n = 2) and hierarchical spatial normalization to MNI152 standard space via FLIRT with cost-function masking (registration failure, n = 272). After exclusion of patients with missing 12-month functional outcome (mRS) or baseline clinical data (n = 194), the final analytical cohort comprised **3,582 patients** with complete neuroimaging, clinical, and 12-month follow-up data (3-month follow-up rate 98.5%; 12-month follow-up rate 97.2%).

## Supplementary Figure 2

**Supplementary Figure 2 | Independent neurotransmitter effects on 12-month outcome (null result).**

Forest plot of standardized regression coefficients (β with 95% CI) for all 17 TLV-orthogonalized neurotransmitter loads (Resid_NT) predicting 12-month modified Rankin Scale (mRS) in the fully adjusted ordinal logistic regression model (Resid_NT + TLV + NIHSS + Age + Sex; N = 3,582). NT systems are ordered by effect size. Two systems reached raw two-sided P < 0.05 (α4β2, NAT) but **no system** survived Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction within the 17-NT family (all q ≥ 0.05; vertical dashed line marks β = 0). This panel documents an honest null result that motivates the moderator/effect-modification framework explored in Fig. 3 and the brain–immune mediation analyses in Supplementary Fig. S5. Raw values are tabulated in Supp_Fig_S2_12m_Independent_Effects.csv.

## Supplementary Figure 3

**Supplementary Figure 3 | Temporal trajectory of neurotransmitter effects across follow-up timepoints.**

Heatmap of standardized effect sizes (β) for the 17 NT systems against modified Rankin Scale assessed at three timepoints: discharge (m3_mRS), 6 months (m6_mRS) and 12 months (m12_mRS). Each cell shows the covariate-adjusted β from ordinal logistic regression (Resid_NT + TLV + NIHSS + Age + Sex). Significant cells (BH-FDR q < 0.05 within the 17-NT family per timepoint) are annotated with their q value; non-significant cells are shaded by raw β only. The pattern shows **acute predominance** (multiple systems FDR-sig at discharge), **attenuation** at 6 months, and **partial re-strengthening** at 12 months for selected pathways (e.g., M1, VAChT, 5-HT1a; see Results). This temporal dissociation supports the interpretation that acute neurochemical disconnection drives early disability whereas long-term recovery is governed by additional moderators (notably systemic inflammation; Fig. 3). All tests are two-sided; n per timepoint reported in Supp_Fig_S3_Temporal.csv.

## Supplementary Figure 6

**Supplementary Figure 6 | Methodological sensitivity analyses: permutation, mRS cutpoint, and atlas-based spatial-null spin test.**

**(A) Permutation test.** Forest plot of observed absolute standardized effect sizes (|β|) for the 17 NT systems against discharge mRS, compared against a non-parametric null distribution obtained by shuffling the outcome label 1,000 times within the covariate-adjusted ordinal logistic regression pipeline. Bars are coloured crimson if the system survived both the parametric (P < 0.05) and the permutation-based (P_perm < 0.05) tests, grey otherwise. Four systems (human_CHA, NAT, α4β2, Lateral_Path) reached concordant significance in both tests. **(B) mRS cutpoint sensitivity.** Heatmap of odds ratios for 5 pre-selected top-ranked NT systems across four alternative outcome dichotomizations: A (mRS 0–1 vs. 2–6), B (0–2 vs. 3–6), C (0–3 vs. 4–6), and D (3-level ordinal 0–2 / 3–4 / 5–6). Cells with `*` indicate two-sided P < 0.05. NAT remained significant across **all four** schemes; α4β2 in 3 of 4; 5-HT6 in 2 of 4; DAT in 1 of 4; VAChT in none, consistent with a robust noradrenergic signal that is insensitive to outcome operationalization. **(C) Spatial-null spin test (Alexander-Bloch 2018).** −log₁₀(P_spin) for each NT atlas, derived by comparing the observed atlas-mRS association strength against a null distribution generated by 1,000 spherical-permutation rotations of the original PET atlas. Vertical dashed line marks P_spin = 0.05. Only the composite cholinergic terminal-density atlas (human_CHA, P_spin = 0.001) reached spatial specificity, indicating that the human_CHA–mRS association is anchored to its specific anatomical topography rather than reflecting a generic atlas effect; the remaining 16 systems showed P_spin > 0.05, demonstrating that no widespread spurious atlas-level inflation contaminates the primary regression results. Numerical values: Supp_Fig_S6A_Permutation.csv / Supp_Fig_S6B_mRS_Cutpoint.csv / Supp_Fig_S6C_Spin_Test.csv.

## Supplementary Figure 7

**Supplementary Figure 7 | Dynamic hsCRP trajectory and the cholinergic-resolution null result.**

Longitudinal corroboration of the effect-modification reading of the CHA × inflammation interaction, using the CNSR-III protocol-defined hsCRP repeat measurements at baseline (BSL), 3 months (M03) and 12 months (M12). The longitudinal sub-cohort comprised N = 1,334 patients with paired baseline + 3-month hsCRP and 12-month mRS; three-time-point sub-cohort N = 820. **(A)** Median hsCRP trajectory ± interquartile range across the three time-points, stratified by tertile of TLV-orthogonalized cholinergic-pathway damage (Resid_human_CHA; T1_low / T2_mid / T3_high, N = 274 / 272 / 274 in the three-time-point sub-cohort). The three trajectories are virtually superimposable at each time-point (BSL medians 2.06, 1.50, 2.15 mg/L; M03 medians 1.25, 1.22, 1.25 mg/L; M12 medians 0.78, 0.80, 0.87 mg/L), directly contradicting the *a priori* hypothesis that cholinergic disconnection prevents hsCRP resolution. Dashed horizontal line indicates the AHA/CDC clinical threshold for sustained inflammation (hsCRP ≥ 3 mg/L; Ridker). **(B)** Distribution of the per-month log-hsCRP resolution slope ([log1p(M03) − log1p(BSL)] / 3) by Resid_CHA tertile in the BSL+M03 sub-cohort (N = 1,334). The highest-damage tertile (T3_high) showed the most negative median slope (T1 = −0.05, T2 = −0.01, T3 = −0.08; Kruskal–Wallis P = 0.004; covariate-adjusted OLS β(CHA → slope) = +0.002 per 1-SD, P = 0.91), i.e. inflammation resolves at least as fast in patients with the heaviest cholinergic damage, opposite in direction to the predicted "sustained-inflammation" pattern. **(C)** Stacked-bar distribution of 12-month mRS by the dichotomous sustained-hsCRP phenotype (M03_hsCRP < 3 mg/L = Resolver, N = 1,026; ≥ 3 mg/L = Sustained, N = 308). The Sustained group carried a higher unadjusted absolute risk of 12-month poor outcome (27.3% vs. 17.4%, χ² P = 1.4 × 10⁻⁴), but the effect was almost entirely captured by baseline hsCRP (adjusted OR = 1.29 [95% CI 0.92, 1.81], P = 0.13 after BSL_hsCRP + TLV + NIHSS + Age + Sex). The fully adjusted ordinal logistic model on the BSL+M03 sub-cohort retained two independent main effects, z(Resid_CHA) (OR = 1.13, P = 0.029) and z(slope) (OR = 1.16, P = 0.032), but the interaction term was effectively null (OR = 1.00; LR test χ²₁ = 0.001, P = 0.98), and the four-cell additive-interaction estimate for Resid_CHA × sustained-hsCRP yielded a sub-additive RERI of −0.63 (95% CI −1.94, +0.32; OR_HH = 1.53 vs. OR_LH = 1.79). Together, panels A–C provide an independent longitudinal corroboration of the cross-sectional mediation null reported in Supplementary Fig. S5: cholinergic disconnection and impaired inflammation resolution operate as two independent prognostic axes rather than as a coupled "release-the-brake → sustained inflammation" cascade. A pre-specified three-time-point sensitivity analysis (N = 820, BSL → M12 per-month log-slope) was run on the same statistical pipeline as Panels A–C and yielded directionally identical estimates (covariate-adjusted β(CHA → slope) ≈ 0, P = 0.68; Resid_CHA × slope ordinal interaction OR = 1.05, P = 0.49; RERI = −0.61, 95% CI −2.31 to +0.63; OR_HH = 1.39 vs. OR_LH = 1.79), with full results written to `out/dynamic_inflammation_triplet/` and figure `FigS7_dynamic_inflammation_trajectory_triplet.pdf`. All P-values are two-sided. Numerical values: `trajectory_descriptive_by_CHA_tertile.csv`, `slope_by_CHA_tertile.csv`, `slope_descriptive_by_CHA_tertile.csv`, `sustained_phenotype_2x2.csv`, `sustained_phenotype_tests.csv`, `dynamic_interaction_ordinal.csv`, `dynamic_interaction_LR.txt`, `additive_interaction_sustained_cells.csv`, `additive_interaction_sustained.csv`.
